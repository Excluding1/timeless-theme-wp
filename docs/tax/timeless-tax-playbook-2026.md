## 1. How tax actually works for a partnership (the big picture)

A partnership is a **"pass-through" (flow-through) entity**. That means:

1. The **partnership itself pays NO income tax.** ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/))
2. The partnership lodges its **own partnership tax return** (it has its own TFN + ABN) showing total
   income, total deductions, the **net profit**, and **how that profit is split** between the partners.
3. That net profit is **divided between Allan and Marko** per your agreed share (default 50/50 unless
   your partnership agreement says otherwise — confirm your split).
4. Each partner then puts **their share** into **their own personal tax return** and pays tax at their
   **individual marginal rate.**

**Critical thing to understand:** you're taxed on your **share of the profit**, whether or not you
actually took the money out of the business. "Partner draws" (money you pay yourselves) are **not**
a deduction and **not** taxed separately — they're just you withdrawing profit that's already taxed.

### Individual marginal rates, 2025–26 (what each of you pays on your share)
| Your share of profit | Tax rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% (tax-free threshold) |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16% |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 30% |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37% |
| $190,001 + | 45% |
Plus 2% Medicare levy on top for most residents.
([FY25-26 rates](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes))

**Why the partnership structure helps you:** splitting the profit across two people means you each get
your own $18,200 tax-free threshold and each start at the lower brackets — so the same profit is taxed
less than if one person earned it all. This is a genuine, legal advantage of the partnership.

## 2. GST & BAS (the quarterly job)

You're **registered for GST** (required once turnover passes $75,000/yr — you're over, so you're in).

- You **charge 10% GST** on every job (the quote app already does this; the tax invoices are compliant).
- Each **quarter** you lodge a **BAS (Business Activity Statement)** that nets off:
  - **GST you collected** on sales = 1/11th of your income, MINUS
  - **GST you paid** on business purchases ("input tax credits").
  - The difference is what you **pay the ATO** (or get **refunded** if you spent more GST than you collected).
- **Quarterly BAS due dates** (standard): Q1 Jul–Sep → 28 Oct · Q2 Oct–Dec → 28 Feb · Q3 Jan–Mar → 28 Apr
  · Q4 Apr–Jun → 28 Jul. (Lodging through a BAS agent usually earns a few extra weeks.)
- **The 10% deposit:** GST is generally triggered when you **receive** the deposit (it's a part-payment,
  not a refundable security bond), so include the deposit's GST in the quarter you receive it. The final
  invoice then only accounts for the balance. The quote app's deposit invoice handles this cleanly.

**The habit that makes BAS trivial:** keep **every** tax invoice — both the ones you issue (income) and
the ones suppliers give you (expenses). Every supplier invoice with GST on it is money back to you.

## 3. Deductions — every legitimate way to pay less (the "hacks")

A deduction reduces your **taxable profit**. Rule of thumb: **anything you buy to earn the income is
deductible.** For Timeless, claim all of these (keep the receipt/invoice for each):

**Direct job costs**
- Coatings and materials (the commercial 3-pack, primers, silicone, grout, etch, masking, drop sheets)
- **Subcontractor payments** — often your biggest deduction. Keep each sub's invoice + their ABN.
- Consumables & PPE (respirators, gloves, suits, sandpaper, tape)
- Tipping/dump fees, replacement parts (e.g. drain covers you supply)

**Vehicle & travel** (choose the method that gives more; your agent will confirm)
- The **van/vehicle itself** (see §4 write-off), plus fuel, rego, insurance, servicing, tyres
- Travel **between jobs** (not home↔first job unless you carry bulky gear)

**Running the business**
- Phone & internet (business-use %)
- Insurance ($10M public liability, tool insurance)
- Software & subscriptions: GHL, Cloudinary, Netlify, Supabase, the domain, Google Workspace, any ads tools
- Marketing & advertising: Google Ads, the website, printing, signage, vehicle decals
- **Bank & merchant fees**, Stripe/pay.com.au fees
- **Accounting/bookkeeping fees** (Xero subscription + agent fees — yes, tax help is tax-deductible)
- Home-office running costs if you do admin from home (power, a share of internet; the ATO fixed-rate
  method is simplest)
- Protective clothing + its laundry
- **Training & licences**: asbestos-awareness course, white card, first aid, and (when you get it) the
  contractor licence and its renewal
- Trade association / union memberships

**What you CANNOT claim:** private/personal use, entertainment, fines, the private portion of anything
mixed-use, and (as partners) your own "wages" or draws. Anything mixed must be apportioned to the
business %.

## 4. Buying gear to "claim it back" — the instant asset write-off

This is the thing you were asking about ("should we buy something before we lose it to tax").

- **$20,000 instant asset write-off:** if an asset costs **under $20,000** (per asset), and you first
  use it by **30 June**, you deduct the **whole cost immediately** that year instead of depreciating it
  over years. Applies to businesses under $10M turnover (you), **new or second-hand**, and you can do it
  for **multiple** assets. It was extended for 2025–26 and, per the **May 2026 Budget**, is being made a
  **permanent** small-business concession from 2026–27.
  ([ATO instant asset write-off](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/depreciation-and-capital-expenses-and-allowances/simpler-depreciation-for-small-business/instant-asset-write-off))
- **Good candidates for Timeless:** a work van (if under $20k — many used vans are), spray/HVLP gear, a
  compressor, extraction/ventilation equipment, a heater (helps winter cure times!), power tools, a
  laptop/phone for the business.

> **The honest reality check** — and this is where bad "tax hacks" trip people up: a write-off is a
> **deduction, not a rebate.** Spending $5,000 on gear saves you roughly **$1,500** in tax (at the 30%
> marginal rate), not $5,000. **You never come out ahead by buying something you don't need** — you're
> spending a dollar to save ~30c. So the rule is: **if you genuinely need the equipment, buy it before
> 30 June to bring the deduction forward. If you don't need it, keep the cash.** Never let a salesperson
> tell you to "buy it for the tax."

**Timing lever:** a small business can also **prepay** some expenses (e.g. 12 months of insurance or a
subscription) before 30 June to pull the deduction into this year, and **defer** invoicing a job into
July to push income to next year — useful if this year was unusually high. Your agent fine-tunes this.

## 5. Super — a quiet, legal way to cut your personal tax

As **partners** you're not employees, so there's **no compulsory super** on partnership profit. But each
of you **can make a personal super contribution and claim it as a personal tax deduction** (a
"concessional contribution"), up to the annual cap (around **$30,000** for 2025–26 — confirm the current
cap). Contributions are taxed at 15% inside super instead of your marginal rate (30%+), so it can save
tax **and** build your retirement at the same time. Worth discussing with the agent once profit is known.

*(When you transition to employees — Marko's plan — you'll then pay Super Guarantee, ~12%, on their wages,
plus PAYG withholding. Different regime; we'll cover it when you get there.)*

## 6. Record-keeping — the foundation everything sits on

The ATO requires you to keep records for **5 years**. Get this right and BAS + returns become quick and
cheap; get it wrong and you overpay tax by missing deductions.

- ✅ **Separate business bank account** (you have one — keep ALL business money through it, no personal mixing)
- **Bookkeeping software** — **Xero** is the tradie standard (it's in Allan's column already); it
  auto-imports bank transactions, tracks GST, and produces BAS in a click. MYOB/QuickBooks are alternatives.
- Track **income** (the quote app + GHL + bank deposits) and **expenses** (every supplier receipt).
- Snap a photo of every paper receipt (Xero/receipt apps store them; the ATO accepts digital copies).
- Reconcile weekly (15 min) so nothing piles up.

## 7. Grants & funding (worth knowing, modest for a resurfacer)

Grants change constantly and most are niche, but the relevant NSW ones:
- **Small Business Fees & Charges Rebate** — up to **$3,000** back on eligible government fees/licences
  (explicitly includes **tradesperson licence fees**) — most useful when you get the contractor licence.
- **Business Connect** — free/subsidised business advisory sessions from Service NSW.
- Export/startup grants exist but don't fit a local resurfacing business.
Check [Service NSW business grants](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/services/business-industries-and-employment/business-grants)
and [nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding](https://www.nsw.gov.au/grants-and-funding) periodically — funds often
run out before the close date.
