The short answer

If your tiles are solid and it is the grout that has cracked, crumbled or gone black, regrouting is enough, and that covers most of the showers we see. Retiling is only the honest answer when tiles are drummy or loose, or the waterproofing membrane behind them has failed. A three-minute check tells you which one you have.

That check is below, and it is the same one we run at the start of every quote. No tools needed beyond your knuckles and your eyes.

The 3-minute self-check

Do these three things before you call anyone, us included.

  1. The tap test. Knock on a spread of wall tiles with your knuckle, especially the bottom two rows in the shower. A solid tile sounds dull and dead. A tile that has lost its bond to the wall sounds hollow, what trades call drummy. One drummy tile in a corner is worth noting. A whole drummy patch is a red flag.
  2. The grout-line inspection. Run your eye, and a fingernail, along the grout lines. Are they cracked, powdery, crumbling out in pieces, or stained black well below the surface? Grout that is failing is obvious once you look at it closely. Missing chunks mean water has been getting behind the tiles for a while.
  3. The silicone check. Look at the flexible seals: wall-to-floor joints, corners and around the screen. Mould inside the silicone (not on it, in it), peeling edges, or gaps mean the silicone is done. Silicone is a consumable and this is the cheapest, easiest part to put right.

Solid tiles plus bad grout is a regrouting job. Drummy tiles or signs of water damage behind the walls is a bigger conversation, and we have that conversation honestly.

When regrouting is enough

Most showers we get called to look terrible and are structurally fine. Grout is the sacrificial part of a tiled wall. It is cement-based, porous, and it sits in the wettest room of the house getting scrubbed, soaked and thermally cycled every day. It fails long before good tiles do.

Regrouting is the right fix when:

  • The tiles pass the tap test and sit flat and firm on the wall.
  • The damage is in the joints: cracked, crumbling, discoloured or mouldy grout.
  • The silicone seals are past it, which almost always goes together with tired grout.
  • Water is escaping at grout-line level, the most common cause of a leaking shower.

Done properly, with the old grout raked out to depth and replaced, and finished with fresh silicone, a regrout makes a shower look close to new and seals the routes water was using to get out. Our shower regrouting service page covers exactly what we do and what it includes.

When retiling is the honest answer

Some showers should not be regrouted, and it does nobody any good to pretend otherwise. New grout on a failed wall is a cosmetic patch over a structural problem, and we would rather lose the job than sell you that. Retiling, usually with new waterproofing, is the right call when:

  • Tiles are drummy or loose across a patch. The bond between tile and wall has failed, often because water has been behind them for years. Grout cannot hold loose tiles on.
  • The waterproofing membrane has failed. Persistent damp in the room next door, a stain on the ceiling below, or swollen skirting near the shower after regrouting-level fixes have been tried, these point below the tiles. A membrane can only be replaced by taking the tiles up.
  • The substrate is water-damaged. Soft, swollen or crumbling wall sheeting behind the tiles means the wall itself needs rebuilding.
  • Tiles are cracked across their faces in numbers. The odd cracked tile can be swapped or repaired. Widespread cracking usually means movement underneath.

If we inspect your shower and find any of this, we tell you, and we tell you why. You get the evidence, not just the verdict.

What professional regrouting involves (removal depth matters)

Here is the thing that separates a regrout that lasts from one that fails in months: how much of the old grout comes out.

Skimming new grout over old grout is painting over rust. It photographs well and it fails fast.

The cheap version of this job scrapes the surface of the old grout and smears a thin layer of new grout on top. There is nothing for the new grout to grip, the old contaminated grout is still underneath, and the joint fails again quickly, often bringing the mould back with it.

A proper regrout means raking the old grout out to a genuine depth with mechanical tools, vacuuming the joints clean, then packing new grout the full depth of the joint. It takes hours longer and it is the difference between a repair and a cover-up. The finished job includes cutting out and replacing the silicone seals as well, because new grout with dead silicone is half a job.

Epoxy vs cement grout: which to refill with

Once the joints are raked out, you have a choice of what goes back in:

  • Cement grout is the standard material, the same family as what came out. It is proven and economical, but it remains porous, which is why it stains and harbours mould over time. Sealing helps, but it needs upkeep.
  • Epoxy grout is a two-part resin grout. It is waterproof rather than porous, so it does not absorb water, resists staining, and gives mould far less to live on. It costs more and is fussier to install, but in a shower, the wettest joint in the house, it is the upgrade that makes sense.

For most shower regrouts we recommend epoxy in the wet zones. The full comparison is on our epoxy grout upgrade page.

Time and mess compared

A regrout is a one-day job for a typical shower. There is dust while the old grout is raked out, but it is managed with extraction, the room is masked, and the mess stays inside the bathroom. You are usually showering again the next day once the grout has cured and the silicone has skinned, and we confirm the exact time on the day.

Retiling a shower is demolition. Tiles off, sheeting checked and often replaced, new waterproofing (with its own mandatory cure days), then tiling, grouting and sealing. One to two weeks without the shower is normal, plus the coordination of two or three trades. Sometimes that is exactly what the room needs. It is just worth knowing the true size of each path before you choose.

What it means for a leaking shower

Most shower leaks start exactly where the self-check pointed: failed grout lines and dead silicone letting water past the tile surface. In those cases, regrouting and resealing fixes the leak without a single tile coming off. That is the good news, and it is the common case.

The exception is a failed membrane, which no amount of surface work will fix. The clues are leaks that continue after grout and silicone are put right, or damp showing up well away from the shower itself. We cover how to tell the difference, and what we do about each, on our shower leak repair page. If you are seeing water where it should not be, sooner is genuinely cheaper than later. Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to regrout than retile?

Yes, considerably. Regrouting is a one-day job by a single trade, working with the tiles you already have. Retiling means demolition, new waterproofing with cure time, new tiles and multiple trades over one to two weeks. If your tiles are solid, regrouting delivers most of the visual result for a fraction of the cost.

How do I know if my tiles need replacing?

Knock on them. Solid tiles sound dull, while tiles that have lost their bond sound hollow, or drummy. Widespread drummy patches, loose tiles, cracked faces in numbers, or damp appearing in adjoining rooms all point to retiling. If tiles are firm and only the grout lines are failing, they do not need replacing.

How long does regrouting take?

A standard shower regrout is a one-day job, including raking out the old grout to depth, refilling, and replacing the silicone seals. Larger jobs, such as a full bathroom including floors, can run into a second day. We confirm the timeframe in your quote, before any work starts.

How long before I can use the shower?

Usually the next day. Fresh grout and silicone both need to cure before getting wet, and the safe window depends on the products used and the weather. We tell you the exact time on the day. Using the shower too early is the fastest way to undo brand-new grout, so it is worth the wait.

Does regrouting stop leaks?

Yes, when the leak starts at the surface, which is the most common case. Failed grout and silicone let water past the tiles, and replacing both seals those paths. If the waterproofing membrane behind the tiles has failed, regrouting alone will not fix it, and we tell you that at inspection rather than after the work.
