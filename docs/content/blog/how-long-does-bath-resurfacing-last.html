The short answer

A professionally resurfaced bath typically lasts 10 to 15 years with sensible care. That is the lifespan. The warranty is a separate thing: ours covers resurfacing for up to 5 years, provided the surface is cared for as set out in our care instructions. Baths that peel within a year were almost always prepared badly, usually with a DIY kit.

Lifespan and warranty get mixed up constantly in this trade, so let us pull them apart properly, and then look at what actually kills a resurface early, because that is the question behind the question.

When people ask how long does bath resurfacing last, what they usually mean is: does this actually work, or will it peel? Fair question. The answer depends almost entirely on what happens before any coating goes on.

Why some resurfaced baths peel in a year

Nearly every early failure we get called to has the same story behind it. The coating did not fail. The bond underneath it did. The usual culprits:

  • DIY kits. Hardware-store bath paint is a one-part product applied with a roller onto a surface that was wiped down at best. There is no proper etch, no bonding agent and no controlled film thickness. Twelve months is a common life for these, less in a bath that gets daily use.
  • No real surface preparation. Old enamel is hard and glassy. If it is not chemically cleaned, abraded and primed, nothing sticks to it for long, no matter how good the topcoat is.
  • Moisture trapped under the coating. Coating over a damp surface, a leaking tap, or an old silicone line that was never removed seals water in. It finds its way out by lifting the coating off in sheets.
  • Coating over a previous failed coating. Spraying over old flaking paint just gives the new layer something loose to hold on to. The old surface has to come off first.

What professional preparation involves (the step nobody sees)

By the time a resurfaced bath is photographed for a website, all you can see is the glossy topcoat. The work that decides its lifespan happened hours earlier and is invisible in the finished job.

On a professional resurface, before any colour goes on:

  1. Old silicone is cut out completely, never coated over.
  2. The surface is degreased and chemically cleaned to strip soap scum, body oils and cleaning-product residue. Oils are the number one enemy of adhesion.
  3. Chips, scratches and rust spots are repaired and sanded back.
  4. The entire surface is mechanically abraded so the glassy enamel has a profile the coating can key into.
  5. A bonding agent is applied, the chemical handshake between old surface and new coating.
  6. Only then is the two-part coating sprayed on, in controlled layers, with proper ventilation and extraction.

The gloss coat gets the photos. The preparation gets the years.

Aftercare that doubles the life

Once the coating has cured, how the bath is treated does the rest. None of this is onerous, but it genuinely is the difference between the bottom and the top of that 10-to-15-year range:

  • Respect the first 24 hours. No water, no cleaning, no standing in it to fix the showerhead. Early water contact is a leading cause of premature failure. We confirm your exact cure time on the day.
  • Use non-abrasive cleaners. Cream cleansers and scourers dull and thin the coating over time. Mild detergent and a soft cloth do the job.
  • No suction mats left in place. Rubber suction mats trap water against the surface and can pull at the coating when peeled off. If you use one, take it out after each bath.
  • Fix dripping taps. Years of a drip landing on one spot will wear a track in any surface, resurfaced or not.
  • Do not cut or place sharp objects in the bath. The coating is tough, but it is not a workbench.

Signs a previous resurface was done badly

Buying a home, or trying to work out what a previous owner did? Here is what a poor resurface looks like before it fails outright:

  • Roller texture or brush marks in the surface. Professional coatings are sprayed.
  • Coating on things that should not be coated: over the old silicone bead, onto tap fittings, over the plughole ring.
  • Edges lifting where the bath meets the wall or around the drain, the first places a bad bond lets go.
  • Yellowing patches within a year or two, common with cheap one-part products.
  • Drips or runs on the vertical faces.

None of these mean the bath is beyond help. They just tell you what is coming, and that the fix is a strip and redo rather than another coat on top.

Can a peeling resurface be redone?

Yes, in almost all cases. The failed coating is stripped or fully abraded off, the surface underneath is repaired and prepared properly this time, and a professional two-part coating goes on. Done that way, the redo performs like a first-time resurface and carries the same warranty.

What we will not do is spray over a failing coating to make it look good for a few months. That job always comes back, and it comes back worse. If a bath has been coated and stripped so many times that another round is throwing good money after bad, we say that in the quote. It is rare, but it happens.

Frequently asked questions

How long does bath resurfacing last?

A professionally resurfaced bath typically lasts 10 to 15 years with proper care. Preparation quality is the biggest factor, followed by aftercare: non-abrasive cleaners, no suction mats left in place, and fixing dripping taps. Our resurfacing work is covered by a warranty of up to 5 years when cared for per our care instructions.

Why is my resurfaced bath peeling?

Peeling almost always means the bond under the coating failed, not the coating itself. The usual causes are DIY kits, poor surface preparation, moisture trapped under the coating, or spraying over an earlier failed coat. The fix is stripping the failed coating and resurfacing properly, not painting over the top of it.

Can you resurface over an old resurface?

Not directly over a failing one. The old coating must be stripped or fully abraded back to a sound surface first, then prepared and primed before the new coating goes on. Done that way, a redo performs like a first-time resurface. Spraying straight over loose old coating fails quickly, every time.

What cleaners are safe on a resurfaced bath?

Mild detergent, diluted in warm water, applied with a soft cloth or sponge. Avoid abrasive cream cleansers, scourers, steel wool and harsh solvent-based products, which dull and thin the coating over time. Rinse cleaning products off rather than letting them sit.
