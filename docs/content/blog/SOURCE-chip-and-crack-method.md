## Acrylic bath, cracked

The important part, and the part no competitor explains: on an acrylic bath **the fix
starts underneath, not on the surface**. A crack in an acrylic base is a symptom of the
base flexing. Filling the crack without stopping the flex means it comes back.

1. Drill a small access point.
2. Pump **structural expanding foam under the base**, so it sits solid and no longer
   flexes.
3. Build the crack up with a **high-strength filler over 2 to 3 coats**, sanding flat
   between each coat.
4. Once the repair is perfectly smooth, **prime** the area.
5. **Resurface** to a gloss finish that matches the rest of the tub.

## Basin, chipped or cracked

No foam stage: a basin is not carrying weight and is not flexing.

1. Fill with **high-strength filler over 2 to 3 coats**, sanding flat between each.
2. Prime.
3. Resurface to a gloss finish matching the rest of the basin.

## Why this is the article's differentiator

- It explains **why** a repair fails when done the obvious way, rather than just listing
  steps. The flex-under-an-acrylic-base point is the whole article.
- It is **first-hand**, from our own quotes, not paraphrased from a supplier brochure.
- It sets up the honest boundary cleanly: we repair cracks, and the earlier blanket
  claim that "a coating cannot fix a crack" was wrong and has been corrected in the
  parked article two. What genuinely cannot be repaired is rust through the steel, a
  base that still flexes after support, and a crack that goes right through the shell.
