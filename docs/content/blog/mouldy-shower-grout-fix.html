Surface mould wipes off with a decent bathroom cleaner and stays gone if the shower dries properly between uses. Mould that returns within a few weeks is living inside the grout or silicone itself, and no amount of scrubbing will fix that. At that point the material needs replacing, not cleaning. Here is how to tell which problem you have.

Why bleach only works for a fortnight

Mould is not a stain sitting on top of your grout. It is a living organism with root structures, called hyphae, that grow down into whatever it is feeding on. Cement grout is porous, full of tiny channels that hold moisture, which makes it ideal territory.

When you spray bleach or a supermarket mould killer on a grout line, two things happen. The chlorine kills and whitens the growth at the surface, which looks like victory. But it barely penetrates the grout, so the root structures deeper in survive. Add a warm shower or two, and the colony regrows from underneath. That is why the black lines reappear in the same places, usually within two to four weeks.

This is not a failure of effort or the wrong brand of cleaner. It is simply the wrong tool once mould is established inside a porous material. You can repeat the cycle every fortnight for years, and plenty of Sydney households do, or you can remove the material the mould lives in.

The silicone rule: mould inside silicone means replace, always

Grout at least gives you a fighting chance while the mould is shallow. Silicone does not, and this is the single most common reason a shower looks mouldy again a fortnight after somebody scrubbed it. Silicone is soft and slightly porous, so growth gets in under the surface where no cleaner reaches. The bead has to come out and a fresh one go in.

Look closely at the silicone beads around the base of your shower, along the screen and in the corners. If you can see grey or black staining under the surface of the silicone, inside the bead itself, no cleaner on the market will reach it. Silicone is a solid, sealed material; whatever grows inside it is protected by the very thing that makes silicone waterproof.

The fix is straightforward: cut the old bead out completely, treat the junction behind it, and run new silicone with a mould inhibitor. It is a quick job with an instant visual payoff, and it is one of the most common things we do. Our mouldy silicone replacement service covers exactly this.

When cleaning is genuinely enough

We regrout showers for a living, and we will still tell you this plainly: a lot of shower mould does not need us. If the growth is recent and sitting on the surface, clean it yourself and keep the money.

Cleaning is genuinely enough when:

  • The mould appeared recently, after a humid stretch, a period of poor ventilation, or a few weeks of the exhaust fan not being used
  • It sits on the surface of the grout and wipes away with light scrubbing, rather than staining deep in the line
  • The grout underneath is sound: firm, not cracked, not crumbling when you run a fingernail along it
  • Once cleaned, it stays gone for a month or more

To do it properly: ventilate the room, wash the grout lines with warm water and detergent first to remove soap scum, then apply a bathroom mould cleaner and let it sit as long as the label allows before scrubbing with a stiff nylon brush and rinsing well. Dry the shower afterwards. Then watch the calendar.

If mould comes back within a fortnight of a proper clean, you do not have a cleaning problem. You have a grout problem.

When regrouting is the fix

The calendar is the test. If black spots return in the same grout lines within two to three weeks of a thorough clean, the colony is living inside the grout, and the honest fix is to remove that grout and replace it.

Other signs that point the same way:

  • Staining that sits deep in the grout line rather than on its surface, often darkest at the bottom of the walls where water lingers
  • Grout that is also cracking, crumbling or falling out in pieces, which means it is holding moisture constantly
  • A musty smell in the bathroom that survives cleaning
  • Mould that reappears even though the bathroom is well ventilated and squeegeed

Proper regrouting matters here. Skimming a thin layer of new grout over old mouldy grout looks fine for a month and then fails, because the contaminated material is still underneath. The old grout has to be raked out to proper depth, the lines cleaned and treated, and new grout worked in fully. Sydney's humidity and ventilation problem

There is a reason this is such a Sydney conversation, and it is not the one most people assume. Mould does not need heat, it needs a surface that stays damp. What decides how fast grout dries after a shower is the humidity of the air it is drying into, and on that measure Sydney works against you for most of the year.

We pulled the Bureau of Meteorology's long-run record for Sydney, Observatory Hill, and counted it ourselves. Mean 9am relative humidity sits at or above 70% for seven months of the year, January through July, peaking at 74%. The annual mean is 69%. The driest month is October at 61%, and even that is not dry.

The practical version: the common belief that this is a summer problem is wrong here. February and June are equally humid at 9am. That is why a shower cleaned in spring is mouldy again by autumn, and why ventilation matters twelve months a year rather than only when the weather feels muggy.

The housing stock makes it worse. Thousands of Sydney apartments have internal bathrooms with no window at all, relying entirely on an exhaust fan, and many of those fans are undersized, clogged with a decade of dust, or vented into the ceiling cavity rather than outside. Older houses often have a window but no fan, and the window stays shut all winter. Either way, the shower stays wet for hours after every use, and wet grout is fed grout.

You cannot change the climate, but you can change how long your shower stays wet. That is most of the battle.

Preventing regrowth

Whether you cleaned the mould yourself or we have just regrouted, the same habits keep it from coming back, and given the humidity figures above they matter all year rather than only over summer. None of this is a product you buy from us, and none of it takes more than a minute a day:

  • Run the exhaust fan during the shower and for 15 to 20 minutes afterwards, or leave the window open until the room is dry
  • Squeegee the walls and screen after the last shower of the day; thirty seconds of squeegeeing removes most of the water the mould would have lived on
  • Clean the exhaust fan cover every few months so it can actually move air
  • Wash or replace shower curtains regularly, as they hold spores against a damp surface
  • Fix dripping taps and shower heads, because a slow drip keeps one section of grout permanently wet
  • When silicone is replaced, use a quality product with a mould inhibitor, and expect to replace it every few years as part of normal bathroom upkeep

What professional mould-proofing looks like

If you are regrouting anyway and never want this conversation again, ask about epoxy grout. Unlike cement, epoxy is non-porous, so there are no channels for moisture to sit in and far less for mould to take hold in. It is not mould-proof, and it still wants ventilation and gentle cleaning.

What it does do is close off the failure this whole article is about, where mould gets down inside a porous joint and cleaning can no longer reach it. Epoxy costs more than cement grout as a job, and on shower floors and lower walls, the areas that stay wettest longest, that is where the extra is worth paying.

On a structurally sound shower it runs like this: rake out the contaminated grout, treat the lines, regrout in epoxy through the wet zones, cut out every silicone bead and replace it with a mould-inhibiting product, then hand over care instructions that take five minutes a week to follow.

Common questions about mouldy shower grout

Why does mould keep coming back in my shower?

Because it is living inside the grout or silicone, not on it. Cleaning removes the surface growth but leaves the roots in the porous material, so it regrows within weeks. Once mould is established inside grout or silicone, the only lasting fix is to remove and replace that material.

Can mouldy grout make you sick?

It can affect some people. Mould spores are a known trigger for asthma, allergies and respiratory irritation, and bathrooms are a common source because of constant moisture. If anyone in the household is sensitive, treat persistent shower mould as a health job as well as a cosmetic one, and fix the cause, not just the stain.

Do I need to regrout or just clean?

Do the fortnight test. Clean the grout properly once. If it stays clean for a month or more, keep cleaning and improve ventilation. If black spots return within two to three weeks, the mould is inside the grout and cleaning will never hold. That is when regrouting becomes the honest answer.

How often should shower silicone be replaced?

In a regularly used Sydney shower, expect silicone to need replacing every few years, sooner if mould gets established inside it. Once you can see dark staining under the surface of the silicone, no cleaner will reach it. Replacement is quick, and new silicone from us is covered for 12 months with correct care.
